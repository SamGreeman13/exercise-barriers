About this project

We interviewed 8 people living in Boston and its surrounding areas about what keeps them from starting or maintaining a fitness routine. Our conversations explored the feelings they associate with the gym and exercise, how they spend their free time, and the mental and psychological hurdles they face.

Most participants named extrinsic barriers — lack of time, energy, or accessible gyms — as their primary obstacles. But the barriers that carried the strongest negative emotions were intrinsic: social media's influence on self-perception, body image concerns, and gym intimidation. These rarely came up as the "main" reason, yet they shaped how people talked about exercise more than anything else.

The most interesting finding was how these two categories interact. Intrinsic barriers don't just exist alongside extrinsic ones — they can create them. Someone who feels intimidated by the gym doesn't just have a psychological barrier; they now have an accessibility problem, because the nearest gym might as well not exist. That interplay is what the visualizations above are designed to make visible.

Téa Adams · Sam Greeman · Northeastern University
