What are METs?

METs (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) is a way to quantify how much energy is needed to complete a task. It is based on a baseline of 1 MET being equivalent to the energy cost of a person at rest. MET-hours is a measure of total energy volume for any activity. For example, sitting at rest for 1 hour is 1 MET-hour.

Typically, any activity under 3.0 METs is considered light; anything between 3.0 and 6.0 METs is considered moderate, and anything above 6.0 METs is considered vigorous.

For adults, the average MET value across an entire day is approximately 1.7 METs (with averages ranging from 1.4 for a lighter day to 2.4 for a more active day). When extrapolated over 24 hours, this means the average daily energy expenditure is about 40.8 MET-hours (ranging from 33.6 to 57.6). For reference, the American Heart Association recommends 2.5 hours of moderate activity (3–6 METs) or 1.25 hours of vigorous activity (>6 METs) per week.
